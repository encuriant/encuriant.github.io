I did a performance on the metro.
2 min read
Published on Sep 15, 2026

On the metro back from work, just as I rose to make my exit, the train made an abrupt halt (#whimsical #whimsy), the jolt driving out of my face all the mystery and nonchalance and indifference that you’re supposed to demonstrate in urban conduct. Now filled with dread, I reflexively reached out for the nearest hanging strap. That instant, somewhere on the Coromandel coast, a howling cyclone fell silent; the fronds of coconut trees ceased all flailing; violent waves and blinding lightnings froze in place. In that cosmic stillness plagiarized from Tamil cinema, a lone electrical impulse materialized in my brain. It zipped a circuitous way through my synapses and dilated my pupils with a realization, “Fuck”.

From the platform, my soul witnessed the magnificent painting of a man pining for a strap descending from the divine ceiling, a strap he would stop short of attaining. When animation returns, his body becomes toy to the laws of physics, motion by motion: 1) a Newtonian force clasps its fingers around the spine and yanks it backward; 2) the torso is flung into a “C”; 3) hinged at the knee, the C rotates and falls on its butt; 4) the feet become airborne with the resulting angular jolt; 5) without a moment spared, a second Newtonian force—the train did a second whimsical jolt of whimsy—grabs the body by its thighs and yanks it too; 6) the “C” is now an “O”; 7) the “O”, gathering momentum, makes one smooth, full rotation. A second rotation may or may not have transpired. It is a blur.

In summary, a man, dressed nicely, donning a sleek backpack, signalling a docile, social-norm-abiding metropolitan lifestyle, has, out of nowhere, without making a fuss or leaving so much as a hint, just stood up and whipped out a beautiful somersault (maybe two) in the middle of a busy San Francisco metro train at 3:45pm, all lasting a total of 2 seconds or less.

Said man now calculates that his wit must defuse the tension that has descended in the wake of his gymnastics routine. Here, the universe forks into two. In one, the man says “That’s all folks! Thanks for coming to my show; tips appreciated.” and bows; motion is breathed back into the cyclone and the coconut trees. In the other, the man, reeling from being freshly assaulted by the universe, while also thrilled from his unforeseen, zero-effort demonstration of athleticism, while also noticing the aghast faces lining both sides of the runway, finds himself disoriented by the emotions inside and outside, chooses to exit and dissolves into the stillness, keeping a face mysterious and nonchalant and indifferent.
