The case of the hole along the right border of the left back pocket.
4 min read
Published on Nov 8, 2026

Much horror was visited upon me with my discovery one night, that my pants had lost a bit of fabric along the right border of the left back pocket, and with that I too had lost bits and pieces of my dignity for god knows however many days that hole had followed me along in public.

I would patch it later, I thought. Except, the hole in the forthcoming days would unravel into a gaping mystery. For exactly a week later, I found in my possession, another pair of pants which too had begun wearing out at the very co-ordinates the previous pair had a hole i.e., along the right border of the left back pocket. Wait a minute. Wasn’t it eight months ago when I had noticed yet another pair of pants with an almost-almost-hole, a pre-precursor to a hole, that too along the right border of the left back pocket, an observation I had then dismissed as mere accident?

That hole, now having amassed scientific significance, loomed over me as a phenomenon that demanded explanation. That is how I willingly became the vulnerable subject of an hour-long interrogation by my friends about my private habits and possessions. Surely, this was not the doings of a rodent. The holes were uncannily identically positioned. Could an alchemic substance of some sort at some point have seared a long hole through a stack of my delicate textiles? None I could think of. Do I place a wallet or a sharp object in my left back pocket? No. In fact, I never use either back pocket for fear of the contents falling out.

One possibility emerged as the most certain. I must possess an unnoticeable asymmetry in my anatomy—besides of course my left-handedness—an asymmetry that with the tedious ticks of a clock over days, weeks or even months, managed to make its presence known through a substantial hole at my backpocket, in in the same way glacial geological forces make theirs known through majestic canyons. Perhaps my anatomical asymmetry even magnifies when I engage in athletic acts like biking, walking, standing and sitting.

Meanwhile, the investigation brought to the fore many more disturbing pieces of evidence. As we scoured through my wardrobe, we found on all my pants and all my shorts of all kinds of fabrics, textures, and silhouettes, under the right lighting and from the right angle, the unmistakable Hole™, at the right border of the left back pocket, each caught at different stages of development, as if by rearranging my wardrobe appropriately, one could create a complete Darwinian chart of the evolution of the Hole™.

The aforementioned pieces of evidence pointed unanimously and incontrovertibly to the hypothesis about my asymmetries. Yet, we were left to grapple with one glaring, irreconcilable exception: none of my gym shorts had been victimized by the Hole™. Not even a trace. At the end of a thorough investigation, I was left baffled, and disheartened by the sudden realization about the hideous state of the near-totality of my wardrobe.

The mystery tossed me around the following six nights. Will I ever find the culprit? What if I simply moved on? Maybe I could patch up or replenish the bottom half of my wardrobe? But what if the new ones too develop the Hole™ because there were unknown forces I hadn’t yet identified and neutralized?

On the seventh day, I made a casual mention of these dire concerns to my dad over a video call. My dad, bypassing all the obvious hypotheses my friends and I had analyzed thus far, put forth an unthinkable yet elegant one: “Do you have anything at the bottom of your backpack?”

A good writer must not use clichés in their writing, but at times, they may be compelled to use one because they have to report what happened with honesty; at least, allow me to say that, at that moment, a shockwave of a certain temperature made its way down my vertebra.

Indeed, as my genius father suspected, there was something at the bottom of my (then, two-year-old) backpack. Besides functioning as the opening to a raincover for the backpack, there was a strip of velcro, which over two whole years, due to my lazy closing of it, had also been stealthily and purposefully consuming the back of each and every one of my pants and shorts in my wardrobe, in proportion to however long I wore each pair while the backpack was worn. Naturally, my workout shorts escaped the terrible fate their brothers suffered.

But why did the velcro take a fancy to the left side? This sinister plot is explained, like many celebrated mysteries, by left-handedness. I always pick up my backpack by my left hand and sling it over my left sholder, letting the right side dangle. This positions the velcro at the co-ordinate of interest, the right border of the left back pocket. Case solved.
